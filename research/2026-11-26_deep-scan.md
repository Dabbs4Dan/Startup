## Pass rule ("FIND")
Passes a 10% false-discovery filter on the first 60% of days AND repeats (p < 0.05, same sign) on the last 40% AND has
the same sign in the other product. Heavy favorites (85–97¢) must also beat their own price on a day-level binomial
test, because a loss-free streak fools a t-test.

## What it says
1. **Nothing we store predicts which way the 10 AM crowd is wrong.** That is the cleanest answer so far to "do our
   current inputs beat the crowd in the morning?": no. New inputs (truckstops, rack) remain the only route there.
2. **How wrong the crowd will be is predictable, but the crowd's own range already shows it.** Useful for bet sizing
   and for skipping days, not an edge by itself.
3. **The reliable results are losers.** 36 of the 45 losing finds are cheap long shots (3–25¢), which lose about 70% of
   the stake. In gas, buying YES at 10 AM loses in every price band (−4% to −30%).
4. **One candidate: the evening crowd overshoots a rising trend.** After the strongest 5-day rises, buying NO
   ("it won't get above this line") at 9–11 PM, all lines priced ≥ 25¢:

   Positive in 5 of 6 gas months. It matches two older observations: the crowd leans 0.89× on yesterday's move vs
   0.73× in truth (Chat 1), and favorites "struggle in fast rises" (Chats 1–2).

## Why it is a candidate, not a strategy
- It was found by a follow-up test on the same days that suggested it. The holdout helps; a forward test decides.
- Rising days come in clumps (May, July, September), so 63 days is fewer independent samples than it sounds.
- Prices are hourly candle quotes. Whether real size fills at those evening prices is unknown (see gap G1/G7).
- Diesel has only 13 such days, all in September.
- **It cannot be traded or forward-tested right now:** both markets have been falling all November (0 rising-most days).
  The falling-side mirror (buy YES after the strongest falls) is weak: gas +2% all days, diesel +6%.
